# patearn — Exit Protocol
## Four-mode decision tree for all active positions

**How to use this file:** At every quarterly review, classify each active holding into exactly one of the four modes below. The classification must be based on the current quarter's re-score and the original entry thesis — not on price performance, not on comfort with the position, not on the current market narrative.

A holding cannot remain in "Re-evaluate" indefinitely. Give it a maximum of 2 quarters, then it moves to either Add or Exit.

---

## MODE 1 — HOLD FULL

**Classification:** Thesis is intact. Business is executing as expected. No red flags.

**Triggers (all must apply):**
- Re-scored NS is within 5 percentage points of entry NS (score not deteriorating)
- ROCE stable or improving quarter-over-quarter
- No Hard Disqualifier has appeared
- Promoter has not sold shares via OFS or block deal
- Concall narrative is consistent with segment-level numbers
- Bear case conditions have not materialised

**Action:** Hold at full position size. Do not trim because the stock has been sideways for 1–2 quarters. Sideways is not broken. Revisit thesis in the following results season.

**Common mistake:** Manufacturing reasons to exit because of discomfort with a flat price. "The sector narrative has faded" or "there are better opportunities" are not thesis-break conditions unless they are reflected in the segment numbers. If the numbers are still executing, hold.

---

## MODE 2 — TRIM

**Classification:** Thesis intact but position size now misaligned due to price appreciation, OR valuation has fully reflected the original thesis.

**Triggers (any one applies):**
- Position weight has grown > 8% of portfolio due to price appreciation alone (rebalance to target weight)
- PE has re-rated to > 25× on normalised (mid-cycle) earnings
- The sector narrative has become very loud — multiple analyst initiations in last quarter, stock appearing in mainstream media as a "multi-bagger idea"
- Entry Math re-run at current price shows implied CAGR < 12% at the same growth assumptions you used at entry

**Action:** Reduce position to original target weight (or to a lower weight if valuation has fully expanded). Record the trim reason. The remaining position continues to compound if the thesis holds. Do not sell entirely — selling the last unit of a confirmed compounder is the most expensive mistake in long-term investing.

**Common mistake:** Selling the entire position because of valuation concern. Partial profit-taking to manage weight is discipline. Full exit at 3× when the business is still executing is premature. Re-run the Entry Math at current price — if you would still buy at 12%+ implied CAGR, the thesis is alive.

---

## MODE 3 — RE-EVALUATE

**Classification:** One or two signals have weakened materially but the thesis is not conclusively broken. Requires active monitoring and a decision deadline.

**Triggers (any one or two apply, but NOT a Hard Disqualifier):**
- ROCE declined in the most recent full year (but not 2 consecutive years — that is Exit)
- Promoter holding flat or declined slightly (but no OFS — that is Exit)
- Export mix growing slower than expected for 2 consecutive quarters
- Management guidance missed in 2 consecutive quarters by > 15%
- One segment showing margin compression while others hold

**Action:** Reduce to half position immediately upon classification. This is not optional — if you are in Re-evaluate, you are sized at half. Set a specific condition in writing under which you will either add back (thesis confirmed: signal recovers in next quarter) or exit fully (signal does not recover in 2 quarters). Maximum 2 quarterly cycles in Re-evaluate status.

**What "specific condition" means:** Not "if things improve." It means: "If ROCE recovers above 20% in Q2FY26 results, add back to full position. If ROCE does not recover by Q3FY26, exit the remaining half."

**Common mistake:** Remaining in Re-evaluate indefinitely. This is a decision delay disguised as prudence. The holding is either worth owning at target size or it isn't. A maximum of 6 months in Re-evaluate status, then the decision must be made.

---

## MODE 4 — EXIT (THESIS BROKEN)

**Classification:** Something has changed that invalidates the original reason for owning the stock. Exit immediately, regardless of price or holding period.

**Triggers — Hard Exits (any one = immediate full exit, no deliberation):**
- Any Hard Disqualifier fires: promoter pledge > 20%, D/E > 2× and rising, CFO negative 2+ years, auditor resignation/qualification, RPT > 15% of revenue
- The specific reason you bought has explicitly reversed: e.g. export mix declining for 3+ consecutive quarters despite management claims, OR segment margin collapsing in the premium segment that was the thesis
- Management credibility broken: 3 or more guidance misses > 20% in last 8 quarters AND segment narrative is inconsistent with numbers
- Sector tailwind formally cancelled or budget reduced > 30%

**Triggers — Soft Exits (require written confirmation before exit):**
- Promoter conducting OFS while simultaneously talking up the story
- CFO/PAT ratio falling below 0.7 for 2 consecutive years
- Receivable days rising > 25 days over 3 consecutive years
- RBI (for financials) or SEBI action against company or promoter

**Action:** Exit the full position. Record the reason in the position log. Do not average down on a broken thesis — this is the single most capital-destructive action an investor can take. A broken thesis does not get better with time; it compounds negatively.

The stock may be re-evaluated from scratch at a later date if the condition that caused the exit is formally resolved. But it must be scored as a new stock with fresh eyes, not as "the one I sold at a loss."

**Common mistake:** Waiting for a better price before exiting a broken thesis. The stock does not know your cost price. When the thesis is broken, every day you hold is a new decision to own a business whose investment case you no longer believe in. Exit first; decide whether to re-enter later.

---

## QUARTERLY REVIEW PROTOCOL

For each active holding, in this sequence:

1. Re-run the 14-pattern score using the latest quarterly results.
2. Note which patterns changed direction (improved or declined).
3. Check all 5 Hard Disqualifiers. If any fires: Mode 4 immediately.
4. Check the original 3-sentence thesis: is each sentence still valid?
5. Check the original 3 exit tripwires: have any been triggered?
6. Classify into one of the four modes above.
7. Verify position size matches mode (Full / Half / Zero).
8. If Mode 3: set or confirm the specific 2-quarter decision condition in writing.

**One rule above all others:** Never reclassify a holding from Mode 4 (Exit) back to Mode 3 (Re-evaluate) because the stock has fallen and "looks cheap." If the thesis is broken, the price being lower does not repair the thesis. A cheap price on a broken business is a value trap.
